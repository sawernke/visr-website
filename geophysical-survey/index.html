Geophysical Survey

VISR provides ground penetrating radar (GPR), magnetometry, and land survey services for research, cultural resource management, and utility location projects throughout Nashville, middle Tennessee, and beyond.

Our team pairs research-grade geophysical instruments with careful post-processing to produce clear, interpretable results, from an initial site visit through a finished report.

Our equipment

  • Ground Penetrating Radar

    We use a GSSI UtilityScan ground penetrating radar with a 350 MHz frequency antenna and depth penetration up to 10 meters. The unit is retrofitted with a Model 626 3-wheel cart for handling in grassy areas and uneven terrain, and equipped with a real-time kinematic GPS unit for simultaneous GPS and GPR collection.

  • Fluxgate Magnetic Gradiometer

    We use a Bartington Grad601 Fluxgate Magnetic Gradiometer with dual sensors. The system offers selectable resolution of 0.01nT or 0.1nT with exceptional temperature stability, and automatically adjusts to reduce power line noise with 50/60 Hz rejection.

  • Total Station & RTK GNSS

    We use Total Stations and RTK GNSS to provide precise locations for marking points of interest, boundaries, and more in a land survey: a Nikon Total Station with two prisms, poles, and a tripod, and Emlid Reach RS and RS2 with poles and a tripod stand.

Processing

GPR post-processing is completed in Radan-7 and ArchaeoFusion, an open-source geophysical data processing software from the University of Arkansas, following four main steps:

  1. Time-zero correction, to adjust for surface reflection
  2. Background removal, to reduce horizontal background noise
  3. Filtering, to remove high and low-frequency noise
  4. Migration, to reduce hyperbolic reflection

Finished raster outputs are exported as time slices and geolocated in QGIS, with interpretations of anomalies digitized atop the rasters.

Magnetometry data is downloaded to TerraSurveyor for data cleaning as necessary to improve data interpretability — including spike removal, denoising, and de-striping — and grid combination. The data is then exported in a raster format to QGIS for geolocation and interpretation.

What we can detect

Ground Penetrating Radar

  • Grave shafts
  • Building footprints
  • Earthworks
  • Utility pipes
  • Geologic features

Fluxgate Magnetic Gradiometer

  • Grave shafts
  • Building footprints
  • Disturbed earth
  • Fire footprints
  • Unexploded ordnance
  • Pipe, cable, and drum locations

Deliverables

Each project produces a detailed report of findings, featuring background information on the site — including historical context and historical maps when accessible — information on the scope and methods of the project, a detailed table of the grid extents, maps of the survey grids and their GPR or magnetometer results, interpretations of the data, and recommendations for further action. We attribute all sources utilized and collaborate with local historians and experts to interpret our data.
