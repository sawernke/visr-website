Geospatial Projects

Using ESRI and free and open source (FOSS) geospatial applications, VISR offers support for geospatial research from conception to publication. We collaborate on a wide breadth of research projects, including mobility and density analyses, georeferencing and digitization, hot spot analyses, creation of web applications for geospatial data collection, creation and deployment of geospatial mobile applications, and configuration of open data portal sites.

Selected projects

  • Movement Corridors Among Persons With HIV in Tennessee

    Movement corridors in the southeastern United States among persons living with HIV in Tennessee.

  • Geographic Variation in Access to Cardiac Rehabilitation

    Collaborators: Meredith Duncan, Natalie Robbins, Steven Wernke, Robert Greevy Jr., Sandra Jackson, Alexis Beatty, Randal Thomas, Mary Whooley, Matthew Freiberg, and Justin Bachmann.

  • Peripartum Mobility in South Africa Among Women With HIV

    Peripartum mobility and maternal/child separation among women living with HIV in South Africa, published December 2023. Collaborators: Kate Clouse, Sandisiwe Noholoza, Sindiswa Madwayi, Megan Mrubata, Natalie Robbins, Carol Camlin, Landon Myer, and Tamsin K. Phillips.

  • Pipeline Opposition Coalitions

    How do coalitions stop pipelines? Conditions that affect strategic action mobilizations and their outcomes, published 2023. Collaborators: David J. Hess, Yu-Ri Kim, and Kaelee Belletto.

  • Guatemalan Coffee Farming Value Creation

    The "Cup of Excellence" Guatemalan coffee competition path, with smallholding coffee producers numbered — Map 7 from Making Better Coffee: How Maya Farmers and Third Wave Tastemakers Create Value, by Edward F. Fischer.

  • Female Jewish Authors' Locations During WWI and WWII

    Evolution of the European landscape compared to the birth and death locations of famous female Jewish authors during WWI and WWII. Collaborator: Allison Schachter.

  • Student Movement Density (Fall 2020)

    A space-time cube model of student movement paths over a two-week period in Fall 2020. Collaborators: Rogers Hall and Ben Rydall Shapiro.
